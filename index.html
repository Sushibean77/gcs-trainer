1 · Method: Check → Observe → Stimulate → Rate

  • Check for factors interfering with communication, ability to respond, and other injuries.
  • Observe eye opening, content of speech, movements of right and left sides.
  • Stimulate: sound (spoken or shouted request), then pressure: fingertip → trapezius pinch → supraorbital notch.
  • Rate the highest response observed.
  • Sternal rub: strongly discouraged by the scale's authors. Pressure behind the jaw: not recommended for routine use.
  • ACS 2024: note whether the stimulus was central or peripheral and keep using the same one on repeat exams.

5 · Interfering factors and NT

  • Pre-existing: language/cultural differences, intellectual or neurological deficit, hearing loss, speech impediment → adapt (interpreter, written questions).
  • Current treatment: intubation/tracheostomy → verbal NT. Sedation or paralysis → wake-up test where safe; otherwise NT.
  • Other injuries: orbital/cranial fracture, dysphasia, hemiplegia, spinal cord damage. With cord injury, test requests using tongue and eye movements.
  • Never record a missing component as 1. Do not report a total when any component is NT; trend the remaining components.
  • Alcohol, drugs, post-ictal state, dementia: rate what you observe and document the confounder. A wrong orientation answer is “confused” whatever the cause.

6 · Common errors

  • Eyes opening to “open your eyes” → E3, not E4.
  • Name and place right, month wrong → V4.
  • Grip without release on request → not proof of M6.
  • Asymmetry → rate the better side; record the deficit separately.
  • Arm pulls away but hand stays below the clavicle → M4, not M5.
  • Slurred but correct answers → V5.

7 · Reporting and what the numbers are used for

  • Individual patient: report components, e.g. “E3 V4 M5”. The sum is for classification and group comparison (ACS 2024).
  • Conventional bands: 13–15 mild, 9–12 moderate, ≤8 severe. ACS 2024 calls this an overly simplistic, one-dimensional approximation.
  • Airway (ATLS): GCS ≤8 usually needs a definitive airway.
  • Transport (ACS 2024): motor <6 or GCS <13 with suspected TBI → highest-level trauma centre. 2021 US field triage red criterion: unable to follow commands (motor <6).
  • ICP monitoring (ACS 2024): GCS ≤8 with structural damage on initial CT; others case by case.
  • Deterioration (GCS authors, citing NICE): urgent reappraisal for a sustained (≥30 min) 1-point drop, a drop of ≥3 in eye or verbal, or ≥2 in motor. Motor changes carry most weight.

8 · Pupils and GCS-P

  • Pupil reactivity score: both unreactive 2, one unreactive 1, neither 0.
  • GCS-P = GCS sum − pupil reactivity score; range 1–15. Example: E1 V1 M4 (6), both pupils fixed → GCS-P 4.
  • It adds prognostic information and does not replace separate reporting of components and pupils.
  • 2025 NINDS clinical working group: record pupils in every patient, separately from the GCS.

  • 2025 NIH-NINDS CBI-M framework: Clinical (full GCS + pupils), Biomarker, Imaging, Modifiers. Still being validated; the GCS components remain its clinical core.
